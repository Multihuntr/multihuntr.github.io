I was thinking about my problems communicating with a colleague who uses a lot of AI. I was thinking about our interactions and how it felt like they were warping reality to justify their use of AI. I realised that, to me, it really sounded like what I'd expect from a drug addict.

We know that using AI is [bad for your brain and critical thinking skills][1]. Whenever you use AI to do something, you are essentially training yourself to not be able to do that thing. We don't need a study to know this; pretty much everyone has experienced it with their maps app on their phone. As we rely on a technology, and offload some task to it, we stop being able to do that task.

It is quite damaging and self-destructive to offload *important* mental faculties to AI. It is not hard to see why, and most people are somewhat aware of the problem. But the people who are using AI too much always seem to have an excuse ready to go to shut down your concern. There's always some mental gymnastics that allows them to justify their use of AI, *even if they acknowledge that it might sometimes be detrimental*.

So I decided to look up a list of lines that alcoholics use to pretend what they're doing isn't that bad to see how they line up with what I hear from people who use AI a lot.

I picked the first [list of lines I found on Google][2] and just went down the list, considering whether there was an analogue.

1. "I'm under a lot of stress". The analogue is: "I just need to get this done". The speaker pretends that it's more important to cope than it is to develop/maintain their own well-being.
2. "I only drink on weekends". The analogue is: "I only use AI when I get stuck". The speaker pretends that there are special times where it's basically ok to overuse AI.
3. "I don't drink in the morning". The analogue is: "I don't use AI for the important stuff". The speaker pretends that the only cause for concern is that they would put out text that is incorrect.
4. "I only drink beer and wine". The analogue is: "I only use AI for the things it's good at". The speaker pretends that they, somehow, know what it is good at, and would never reach for AI when thinking for themself would have been possible and better.
5. "Alcohol gives me confidence". The analogue is: "It makes me more productive". The speaker pretends that AI is making them better as a way to deflect from the negatives.
6. "I have never blacked out". The analogue is: "I have never lost a skill because of AI". The speaker pretends as if never having had a very bad outcome makes it ok to continue. They may also be mistaken.
7. "Everyone else is doing it". The analogue is: "Everyone else is doing it".
8. "My addiction does not affect anyone". The analogue is: "My addiction does not affect anyone". The speaker pretends as if their AI addiction doesn't degrade/cheapen your interactions with them.
9. "I can't afford rehab". Not sure if there's an analogue to this one.
10. "You're not a doctor, you can't diagnose me". The analogue is: "You're not a tech genius, you can't tell that it's definitely a bad thing". The speaker pretends that you have no authority to complain. As if it isn't your relationship that is being strained by their problem.

To be honest, I wasn't expecting these to line up so well. In most of these, it's a one-to-one comparison. The AI addicted brain is whispering all the same kinds of messages to convince them that actually, it's fine, there's no problem here. The same little lies that, individually don't feel that bad, until you wake up and realise you can't do anything without asking the AI first. The first and only tool that you can think of is an AI.

I know that people have complained about every new technology. I don't know that I have a strong argument to show that I'm not falling prey to those same mental traps, either. I can only tell you what it looks like to me. It looks to me like many people are going to lose a lot of skills, and they're going to be harder and harder to reach.
